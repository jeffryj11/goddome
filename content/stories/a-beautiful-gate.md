A Beautiful Gate *Writing my heart out — Jeanna’ Mead* > “Now a man who was lame from birth was being carried to the temple gate called Beautiful, where he was put every day to beg from those going into the temple courts.” > *(Acts 3:2)* I had never noticed this verse before — I had skimmed past it in my Bible readings and morning devotionals. But this time, the words jumped out at me. Tears filled my eyes as I thought about what the word **“beautiful”** means to me — and how different it feels these days. **“Look at us!”** Here, Peter tells the lame man to look up at him and John — and suddenly, it made perfect sense to me. The lame man was embarrassed, even ashamed. He most likely kept his head down, gaze lowered, posture defeated. He had spent his whole life begging for scraps — scraps of money, scraps of kindness, scraps of anything to get by one more day. He didn’t expect to be treated with respect. He didn’t expect anyone to REALLY see him. He didn’t expect to be looked at as a man created by God, known and loved. He was most likely seen as a **problem** — maybe even an eyesore. Maybe even a burden. The scripture says someone carried him to the gate every day. I wonder who carried him? Did they leave him for hours without food or water? Did they stay nearby to protect him? Did they let him sit alone so strangers would feel more mercy? Did he even have a home to return to? The Bible doesn’t tell us those details, but it tells us what we *do* need to know — what makes my heart dance with joy: > **“Silver or gold, I do not have, but what I do have I give you. > In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth — walk.”** Then Peter took him **by the right hand** and **helped him stand**, and instantly his feet and ankles became strong. Let that soak in. This man was **touched.** Not just spoken to — *touched.* As a massage therapist, a writer, a woman living with hearing loss, and a granddaughter of a touch-centered Mema, touch is my love language. It is who I am. It is what I do. So reading these words is like a balm to my soul. Peter **helped him up.** Simple. Sacred. Holy. To help someone rise means your heart moves first. Your eyes see them. Your body acts. You become a vessel of love — the very thing God created us to be. > “We are God’s handiwork, created in Christ Jesus to do good works, > which God prepared in advance for us to do.” > *(Ephesians 2:10)* How often do we see someone’s hardship as our invitation to be a blessing? Not to judge. Not to ignore. But to say, “Here is my chance to share what God gave me.” That’s what the last four years have been for me. My own **Beautiful Gate.** My own wilderness. My own battleground. It has changed me — from the inside out. --- ## **He Became Strong** The man was lame his whole life, and then **instantly** he became strong. That’s how we should believe in God’s power. Not timidly. Not cautiously. But boldly — knowing He can change everything in an instant. > “Be strong, all you people of the land, declares the Lord, and work. > For I am with you.” > *(Haggai 2:4)* Once the man’s legs became strong, he **jumped** and **praised God.** Worship is work — holy work. Sometimes we have to stay strong while we wait on the Lord, but waiting is not wasting. Waiting prepares our hearts. Waiting deepens our faith. Waiting grows our roots. The man didn’t give up — he returned to the gate day after day. And when the moment came, he looked up, reached out, and rose. We must do the same. --- ## **They Recognized Him** > “They recognized him as the same man who used to sit begging at the temple gate called Beautiful…” > *(Acts 3:10)* Here is where I get raw. Four years ago, I got sick — really sick — with Covid. It became a wilderness I never expected. My small Texas town grew fast, but it’s still small enough that people know you — sometimes by what you do, sometimes by who you’re related to, and sometimes by what they think they know. I was “the gym girl.” Always at Legends. Always at Anytime Fitness. Always working out. But I was also hiding. Hiding my profound hearing loss. Hiding the exhaustion. Hiding the self-doubt. Hiding how hard it was to keep pretending I could hear what I couldn’t. People told me I would fail. That a “half-deaf woman” couldn’t succeed in business. So I pushed myself harder. Proving. Performing. Pretending. Then Covid hit. My legs wouldn’t hold me. My vision blurred. My speech slurred. My lips went numb. I didn’t recognize the woman in the mirror. And everything I built fell apart. But God met me at **my Beautiful Gate** — again and again and again. My husband and my children showed me love and patience I didn’t think I deserved. I stopped being ashamed of my deafness. I stopped believing I had to be “enough.” I stopped working to earn love. I started *receiving* love. Real love. God’s love. I became weak so I could feel His strength. I lost my vision so I could gain His. I stopped seeing myself as a wildflower and began seeing myself as a **tree**: > “But I am like an olive tree flourishing in the house of God; > I trust in God’s unfailing love forever and ever.” > *(Psalm 52:8)* --- ## **By Faith in the Name of Jesus** The man at the Beautiful Gate was healed by faith in the name of Jesus. I believe the same for me. > “He who began a good work in you will carry it on to completion…” > *(Philippians 1:6)* I believe in faith. I believe in Jesus. I believe He is healing me — body and soul. The wilderness is ending. A new season is beginning. The gate is opening. And I will praise my Savior all the day long.

If you missed those, you can learn more about the themes we've explored here and here.) The Power of Intercession You may be concerned about someone in your life today. Perhaps even several people! You love and care for them deeply. You want God to move in a specific way in their life. Those feelings may be leading you to pray for them. Intercessory prayers are the prayers we pray for someone else. When we intercede for someone in prayer, we come to God and plead on their behalf, as if we are standing between them and God. Often, those intercessions result from us realizing we cannot do what we want to do for them. We know that only God can work in a specific way or area, so we come to God on their behalf. I love what Richard Foster says about intercession. "If we truly love people, we will desire for them far more than it is within our power to give them, which will lead us to prayer. Intercession is a way of loving others." It's painful to watch someone you love on a destructive path and feel powerless to help. In moments like those, here's the principle I want you to remember: Intercession is the overflow of our love for people and our limits as people. There are limits to what you can do for the people you love. You cannot make choices for them and you are not able to change their circumstances. Therefore, you go to God on their behalf. We pray for the people we love first and foremost by letting God know that we love them and that we've reached our limit of what we can do for them. When we confess our limits as people and declare our trust that God is more than able to move in the lives of those we love, we put ourselves in a position to see God move powerfully. As Paul writes, "Carry each other's burdens, and in this way you will fulfill the law of Christ" (Galatians 6:2 NIV). Sometimes the most potent way we carry someone's burden is through prayer. If you embrace this vision of intercession, you could experience what George Müller did. By the end of his life, the evangelist and advocate for orphans recorded over 50,000 answered prayers (30,000 answered the same day) as a testimony that God answers prayer even in challenging circumstances. Müller's primary goal in documenting these answers was to serve as a testimony to other believers. He wanted to show them that they could experience similar answers in their own lives.
