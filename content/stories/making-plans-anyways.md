**By Jeanna’ Mead** A few days ago, I heard this song about God’s plans, and several lines hit me straight in the heart: > **If it’s not good, then You’re not finished yet > You have good plans > It’s true in the palace and it’s true in the pits > If You’re in control, then I’m lifting my hands > You have good plans > You’re going to lead me from glory to glory > You’re not done writing the BEST of my story** I started writing this on Tuesday afternoon. But on Wednesday, September 10, 2025, a shot rang out and a man was killed because of what he believed in and spoke about. Since then (it is now Saturday, September 13), so much has been said and done — things that broke my heart and things that restored my faith in God and in people. **He is not done writing the best of our stories.** But before all this happened, I sat down to write about something else — the heartbreaks God allows to shape us, break us, and bring us closer to Him. --- ## **Heartbreak Happens to Every One of Us** Several years ago, I asked God to break my heart with the things that break His. I didn’t realize how deeply He would answer that prayer. I was becoming self-centered, comfortable, tucked inside my circle of family, friends, and clients. I wanted God to use me, but I didn’t want Him to *touch* the places that needed changing. And He did. He broke my heart for what breaks His. He opened my eyes. He stretched my compassion. He pushed me out of my little world. And now… I’ve learned to be careful what I ask for — because I just might get it. So now I’m asking for what I truly want — not from fear, but from trust. --- ## **A Father Who Loves to Hear From His Children** A friend of mine got a voicemail from his daughter. In her tiny, trembling voice she said: > **“Daddy, I need you. Daddy, please can you call me? Daddy, I love you and I need you. Please text me back.”** Of course he dropped everything and called her immediately. And it hit me… Our Father in heaven says the same: > **“If you, then, though you are evil, > know how to give good gifts to your children, > how much more will your Father in heaven > give good gifts to those who ask Him?”** > *(Matthew 7:11)* Oh sweet Jesus, this hits my heart so hard. I am a momma. When my children ask me for anything, I feel honored and valued. Even when I don’t have all the answers, the fact that they *came to me* fills my heart. That is exactly how God feels about us. He wants to hear from me. He wants to hear from you. He wants us to come running with our hands outstretched, hearts wide open, eyes dancing, saying: **“Daddy, I love You. Daddy, I need You. Daddy, help me.”** He created us for that. He delights in that. That is the *best* story ever. **His plans for us are good. And He is a good, good Father.** --- ## **A Song on Repeat in My Heart** These lyrics keep circling through my mind: > **You’re a good, good Father > It’s who You are > And I’m loved by You > It’s who I am** I know for certain that what I think about shapes my life. So I am choosing to think about: - His love - His goodness - His plans - His promises - His story being written in me He is not done writing the best part of my story. And I pray — with everything in me — that you feel the same. **He has good plans. He’s not finished yet.**

By Jeanna’ Mead A continuation of words written by another.. sometimes what I read inspires me in such a way that I feel I must write my thoughts, too. This is what I read in a Youversion devotional. The Whispering Spot When I first toured the Capitol more than two decades ago, our guide revealed a secret that wasn’t really a secret: the whispering spot. He stood on one side of Statuary Hall while our tour group stood on the other side. Then he spoke in a whisper, and sure enough, we could hear the echo of his voice all the way across the room as if he were mere inches away. When I look through the Bible, I see whispering spots everywhere. For Jacob, it was Bethel. For Moses, it was a burning bush. For Samuel, it was the tabernacle at Shiloh. For David, it was the cave of Adullam. For Elijah, it was Mount Carmel. For Daniel, it was an upstairs window facing Jerusalem. For Jonah, it was the belly of a whale. When I survey Scripture, I see God showing up in strange places, at strange times, in strange ways. And I don’t think anything has changed. God still turns appointments into divine appointments. He still uploads desires, opens doors, and inspires dreams. He still speaks through promptings and people and pain. And just as he did for Moses, He can turn any patch of ground into holy ground. Listening doesn’t happen by default; it happens by design. You have to seek solitude, seek silence. You have to ruthlessly eliminate distractions. And you have to turn some voices down or tune them out altogether. It might be as innocent as talk radio or as innocuous as social media. Why not turn off the radio and talk to God during your commute? Or fast from social media for a season? Or take a silent retreat? I don’t want to overspiritualize the importance of a whispering spot, but I don’t want to underspiritualize it either. Even if you take spirituality out of the equation, you need a space or place to get some peace and quiet. If you live in a city, as I do, it’s not easy. And if you’re a parent of little children, it might be ten minutes during naptime. Your whispering spot will be as unique as you are, but you need to find a time and place to hear the still, small voice of God. Do you have a “whispering spot” in your life right now? If not, where might that spot be? If you do, how has God whispered to you there? Here is my own words about this whispering spot. Last night, my Riven spent the night with me and we went through treasures - my hope chest had collapsed and I had to take everything out and has we looked through pictures of me as a child holding dolls and then I placed my moms childhood doll and then Mema’s into her hands and I felt a rush of generational love sweep over us all. I showed her the thimble I had found years ago underneath my bed. Years after Mema went to dance in glory, and after we had changed beds twice - a sure sign from God to remember where I came from and who I am. He’s been whispering to me. He’s been getting close enough for me to read his lips and He keeps placing devotionals and scripture in my hands and using things to get my attention I just shake my head and say, ”Okay, Jesus, what are you telling me?” and just like that, “Who raised you? Who inspired you? What would Mema do?” And damn, if I don’t start thinking about prune cake and pinto beans and the open invitation she had. I can make all kinds of excuses and then I hear her say “so what?” So what?! There’s that whisper. So what if I mess up? So what if I have scars showing and so what it I’m walking without grace? So what if my home isn’t fancy and so what if my paint needs another coat ? So what if some people think I’m bat shit crazy and so what if my words aren’t always pretty? So what if some people don’t have anything to do with me anymore? So what if it’s hard to start over again? There’s no reason to be acting ugly and no reason not to get up and dance this song That’s how the Spirit whispers to me- with music, with memories, with moments and with words and treasures “I’m going to do a new thing Do you not perceive it? I will make streams in the desert And a way in the wilderness.” I’m listening. I’m reading His lips. I’m going to do what He’s telling me to do So what if it’s crazy Jesus made me that way. And God made sure I was raised by a woman that talked sense into me over and over, again. Now I have to whisper those stories to Riven and write them for people like you to read. It’s a whisper in your soul.
