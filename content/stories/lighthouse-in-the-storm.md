The coastal winds whipped violently against the rocky cliffs of Cape Hope. Waves crashed fifty feet into the dark granite ledge, filling the midnight air with sea spray and roaring thunder. In the small keeper's cottage below the beacon, Captain Thomas checked the lens for the hundredth time.

Outside, the darkness was absolute. A fierce tempest had cut all radio contact with incoming harbor vessels. Inside, Thomas felt the creeping chill of helplessness. His daughter, Anna, sat by the oil lamp softly humming an ancient hymn of sanctuary.

> "The storm does not diminish the light, Thomas," Anna whispered softly. "It is the storm that makes the light necessary."

Realizing that fear was a thief of purpose, Thomas refueled the oil reservoir, calibrated the prism gears, and stepped out onto the gale-swept balcony. Hour after hour, the bright beam pierced through the dense fog and rain.

At dawn, three fishing boats anchored safely in the quiet harbor basin. The captains came ashore with trembling hands, thanking God for the steadfast light that guided them through the impenetrable blackness. When the world rages around us, God's promise stands as our unwavering beacon.
