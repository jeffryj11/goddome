--- title: "Common Ground" slug: "common-ground" date: "2025-02-26" summary: "Jeanna’ reflects on how every soul is made in God’s image, every heart is infected by sin, and every person is invited into Jesus’ love — even in a divided world." tags: ["unity", "faith", "love", "humility", "community"] author: "Jeanna’ Soul" --- Oh, sweet Jesus, here You go again, placing things in my hands with a grin and saying, **“What cha gonna do about this, Jeanna’? Do you see what I’m trying to tell you?”** This morning, He made sure I opened up my Bible app to this devotional about a touchy matter — **Common Ground.** > Yesterday, we took a look at our differences. Today, we’re looking at what we all have in common. > **We’re all images. > We’re all infected. > We’re all invited.** > We are all made in the image and likeness of God — with dignity, value, and purpose. Every single life matters. > We are all infected by sin — which blinds us to the image of God in others and makes us small, spiteful, or self-centered. > We are all invited — Jesus asks each of us, “Do you want to be made well?” So here I am, sipping coffee and writing my heart out again because this devotional hits real close to my heart — my broken heart, my scarred heart, and my wide-open heart. I’ve had just about all I can handle of the ugliness I keep seeing — families, friends, strangers — calling each other names, shaming, excluding, bashing each other for who they voted for or didn’t vote for. **Lord, almighty, I quit taking anything seriously if it starts with words like:** *“libtards,” “Demonrats,” “Orange Man,” “Muskrat,” “Trumpist”…* You know why? Because deep down, this is what I believe: **Everybody is a little scared. Everybody is a little wrong and a little right. Everybody is a little overwhelmed and a little hurt.** All of us. And when I see someone making an ugly remark about a person they don’t even know, it riles me up. Sometimes I *do* know who they’re talking about — the precious son of a beloved friend, the daughter of someone else, the survivor of assault, the one who fought addiction, the “holy roller” hiding shame, the hurting, the bitter, the lonely. Every single one of us fits one of those categories at some point. **Damn straight. It’s that simple.** Like the devotional says — we’re all images. ALL. Period. Which brings me to one of my favorite quotes from C.S. Lewis: > **“You don’t have a soul. You *are* a soul. You have a body.”** Get it? Every single person is a soul — created in the image of God — known, loved, seen, and valued by Him. He thinks we’re worth: - being included in His Kingdom - being invited into His glory - being sought after and pursued - being wanted and cherished Think about it. Take a deep breath and think about it. Your soul is so sacred, so precious, that Jesus gave His life for it. And Satan does everything he can to take your soul away — to divide, isolate, cause trouble, and create chaos. A damn good job, indeed. Instead of gathering at tables, we slam doors. Instead of talking face to face, we post ugly comments online. Instead of offering grace, we pile on shame. We do. And it’s a crying shame. I reckon it breaks the heart of The Father, because He’s looking at each one of us and saying: **“That’s MY child. I created that one. I formed that baby — fearfully and wonderfully. And I love them.”** He doesn’t play favorites. He doesn’t exclude. He loves them ALL. So shouldn’t we? Shouldn’t I? Shouldn’t you? I’ll tell you something else — I don’t have it in me to walk around acting like I’m better than anyone else. I’m not. Never have been. Never will be. There’s this saying: > **“Don’t judge those who sin differently than you.”** Isn’t that what we do when we act ugly? When we talk dirty? When we call names? When we point fingers? Remember this: When you point one finger at somebody, **three** point back at you. And those three fingers ought to remind you: **Image. Infected. Invited.** Do your part. Listen to the heartbeat that God gave you — the same heartbeat He placed in every person He created — and open your heart to love the way He loves you, loves them, loves all of us. There you go. Sweet Jesus… I’m listening to You talk. I will bake and invite. I promise. I’ll invite. It may just be me and You, Jesus — but that’s more than enough. And I have a feeling You already have the rest up Your sleeve. You’re just waiting to see how I’m gonna handle this.
